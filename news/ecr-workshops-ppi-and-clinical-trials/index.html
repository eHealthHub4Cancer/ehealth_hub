Early Career Workshops Empower Next Generation of Health Researchers

October 13, 2025 · KATIE CREMIN

Earlier this month our team was delighted to host a series of Early Career Researcher (ECR) Workshops in collaboration with AICRIstart. These interactive sessions brought together PhD students, postdoctoral researchers, patient advocates, and clinical experts to reflect on the evolving role of public and patient involvement (PPI) and clinical trials in shaping impactful health research in Ireland.

Workshop 1: Embedding Public and Patient Involvement in Research

The first workshop, facilitated by Dr Emma Dorris, Engaged Research Manager and co-lead of the PPI Ignite Network @ UCD, focused on strengthening understanding of Public and Patient Involvement (PPI) within laboratory-based and data science research.

The PPI Ignite Network aims to foster a culture of meaningful involvement across Ireland’s health research landscape. Through presentations, small-group exercises, and reflective discussion, participants explored why PPI matters, how it can enrich preclinical and translational research, and practical ways to embed PPI at all career stages, even starting small.

We were particularly grateful to the patient advocates from UCAN (United Cancer Advocates Network) who joined the session and shared invaluable lived experience and insights. Their openness and perspective brought research impact into sharp focus and encouraged participants to think beyond the lab bench and data set.

Workshop 2: Understanding the Lifecycle of a Clinical Trial

The second workshop was hosted in collaboration with Cancer Trials Ireland (CTI). The day offered participants a unique, behind-the-scenes look at how a cancer clinical trial develops, from initial idea through to publication and patient benefit.

CTI staff led an engaging session on “The Lifecycle of a Clinical Trial”, walking participants through each stage: from concept design and ethical approvals to data infrastructure, quality and safety oversight, and dissemination of findings. Real-world case studies provided practical context, highlighting both challenges and innovative solutions in managing academic trials.

In the afternoon, PhD and postdoctoral researchers presented their own work to CTI staff and invited investigators, receiving valuable feedback and encouragement for future collaboration. The day concluded with an open Q&A session, sparking discussion about career pathways, the role of multidisciplinary teamwork, and how ECRs can contribute to Ireland’s growing clinical research ecosystem.

Fostering Reflection and Connection

Across both workshops, a clear theme emerged: the importance of creating space for early career researchers to connect with peers, reflect on the “big picture,” and consider how their research can have real-world impact.

These sessions were made possible through the support of the HEA, and we extend sincere thanks to Dr Emma Dorris, the PPI Ignite Network @ UCD, the Cancer Trials Ireland team, and all participants who contributed their time and insights.

Together, we are building a community of researchers who are not only excellent scientists but also engaged, reflective, and motivated to make a difference for patients and the public.
